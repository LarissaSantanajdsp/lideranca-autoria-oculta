# Report: Leadership Messages Under Hidden Authorship (AI, Human, and Augmentation)

> Technical synthesis of the Master's thesis (MPA) by Larissa Angélica Páscoa Santana (founder of the communication school CALOR and researcher at FGV EAESP), advised by Prof. Dr. Paul Ferreira and evaluated by an examination committee composed of Prof. Dr. Fábio Josgrilberg (FGV EAESP) and Profa. Dra. Tatiana Iwai (Insper), approved with distinction in November 2026 (CEPH/FGV Ethics Approval No. P.030.2025). A 3×2 factorial experiment conducted in May 2026 with 200 valid participants in Brazil via the Prolific platform, testing instructional leadership messages written by humans, by artificial intelligence (ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini), or through human–AI co-authorship (augmentation), with and without charismatic leadership tactics (Antonakis et al., 2011; 2022), under hidden authorship.

## Executive Summary and Key Findings
- **Inability to Detect AI:** Only 6.1% (n = 4) of participants exposed to texts written 100% by AI (N = 66) identified the algorithmic origin as "An AI system"; 54.5% (n = 36) attributed the text exclusively to "A person", 24.2% (n = 16) to "A person using AI", and 15.2% (n = 10) answered "Cannot tell". Across the total sample (N = 200), 51.0% (n = 102) attributed authorship to "A person", 27.0% (n = 54) to "A person using AI", 9.5% (n = 19) to "An AI system", and 12.5% (n = 25) selected "Cannot tell". Fisher's Exact Test indicated no statistically significant association between actual message source and participant-perceived authorship.
- **Perception of Purpose and Meaning (chi-square = 8.24, p = 0.016):** When asked about the impact of the instructional message, 55.6% (n = 35) of participants who received exclusively human-written text (N = 63) indicated that the message made the task seem "more important / meaningful", compared to 36.4% (n = 24) in the pure AI group (N = 66) and 32.4% (n = 23) in the Human + AI co-authorship group (N = 71).
- **Indifference to the Message (p = 0.047):** The response "the initial message did not affect me" was recorded by 4.8% (n = 3) of participants in the Human group, 18.2% (n = 12) in the pure AI group, and 14.1% (n = 10) in the Human + AI group.
- **Operational Performance and Statistical Boundary:** In the tests conducted, no statistically significant differences were observed in delivery volume (Beta-Binomial GLMM, p > 0.21) or technical accuracy (Quasi-Binomial GLM with CR2 cluster-robust standard errors, p > 0.84) across groups, nor was empirical support found for any of the six formulated hypotheses (H1a to H3b; p > 0.50 for main effects and interactions). Saturation at the task's upper limit (ceiling effect of 31 fragments in 10 minutes, reached by 86.1% to 97.0% of participants per group) and the absence of a formal non-inferiority test prevent concluding that human and algorithmic leadership yield equal operational outcomes or claiming parity between the messages produced by the groups.

## Practical Utility and Theoretical Contributions
- **Transparency Rules (Disclosure) and Managing Algorithm Aversion:** The low spontaneous detection rate (6.1%) aligns with Choudhury, Vanneste, and Zohrehvand (2024, *The Wade Test*: 59.0% overall accuracy and 56.6% for AI responses, alongside algorithm aversion effects upon labeling), suggesting that organizations should establish institutional policies defining when AI usage in internal communication must be disclosed and how human oversight should be structured.
- **Allocating the Human Leader to Purpose-Driven Messages:** The findings suggest careful reflection on using AI when the communication goal is tied to purpose and meaning, connecting with charismatic leadership and signaling literature (Antonakis et al., 2011; 2022; Ernst et al., 2022).
- **Theoretical Framework:** Bridges the CASA paradigm (*Computers Are Social Actors*; Reeves & Nass, 1996; Nass & Moon, 2000) with AI-Mediated Communication (AI-MC; Hancock, Naaman & Levy, 2020; Hohenstein et al., 2023) and empirical evidence on human attribution bias (Jakesch, Hancock & Naaman, 2023).
